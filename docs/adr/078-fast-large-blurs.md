# ADR-078: Fast large blurs

- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Status:** Accepted

## Decision

Fast large blurs; parallel, damage-aware backdrop filters (phase 6, T5; amends ADR-028's blur passes, ADR-045's "a list that uses it rasterizes on one goroutine" and ADR-061's backdrop closure). **Downsampled blur:** a device standard deviation s gets a factor f, the largest power of two (at most 64) whose full-resolution support 3f(m+1) + 3f/2 - 1 plus the rounded offset fits the reach 3s + \|offset\| (from about s = 21; f is about s/10). The surface is averaged over f x f blocks aligned to device coordinates (8.8 fixed point), the blocks are blurred with three passes of a fractional box (2m+1 samples plus a weight alpha at each end, 24-bit integer weights) whose variance is that of the full-resolution three-box kernel divided by f², minus the 1/4 that averaging and linear interpolation add, and the result is interpolated linearly back to the pixel centers with exact integer weights, rounded once. Smaller s keep the full-resolution kernels, bit for bit. **Backdrop filters** compute only the output pixels they draw, from that rectangle grown by the filters' reach (a mirrored pixel lies within it). `raster.BackdropRegion` grows a region by what each spreading backdrop filter reads to compute its output inside it (its read bounds within its reach of that output), from the last filter to the first, and `Render` renders the grown region on a reused scratch copy whose other pixels need not be exact. So core presents and rasterizes its damage rectangles ungrown (the grown area only counts toward the full-frame fallback), and its damage closure damages, in list order, only the output within a filter's reach of the damaged pixels it reads (`raster.BackdropOutputRegion`; the whole output inside a filter layer). **Parallelism:** a list whose spreading backdrop filters all lie outside layers is rendered in bands, one per worker, that wait for each other at each such filter. The last band to arrive computes the filter's output once over the region with a work pool (the blur's rows, column strips and block rows split among goroutines) while the others wait, then every band draws its rows. Other such lists render on one goroutine, with the pool. Pixels do not depend on worker counts, band splits or regions. **Drop shadows:** `drop-shadow-*` and `drop-shadow-[...]` blur with a standard deviation equal to the blur radius (Filter Effects 1's `stdDeviation="[radius]"`, what Chromium draws), not box-shadow's radius/2

## Rationale

Chromium's CPU blur stays a full-resolution three-box blur even at s = 128 (measured), so the low-resolution kernel matches its variance and shape family: a Gaussian with the same variance was 2-4 levels off, the fractional boxes are within 1 level of the full-resolution blur for s = 21-256, offsets and odd origins, and `TestChromiumBlur` (blur-xs ... 3xl, backdrop-blur-xs ... 3xl, drop-shadow-xs ... 2xl at scales 1 and 2) gives the same maxima as before (at most 3 levels away from anti-aliased edges), means within 0.002. Truncated Gaussian taps at low resolution were as accurate but cost O(s/f) per value; fractional boxes cost O(1) but need s/f of about 10 to fit the reach. Measured on a shared Core Ultra 5 125H limited to 4 cores, base and new interleaved (medians): a 1920x1080 glass frame (gradient, 2,000 glyphs, full-width backdrop-blur-xl panel) 46 -> 16 ms rasterized (37 ms on one goroutine; core's full frame 54 -> 21 ms); a 200x200 change under the panel 40 -> 5 ms (core frame 47 -> 5.7 ms; the presented damage 1920x402 -> 348x348 px, rasterized with the 496x402 px it reads); blur-3xl on a 448 px glow 19 -> 9 ms at scale 1 and 72 -> 25 ms at scale 2; `examples/shapes` at scale 2 245 -> 107 ms (layout included). Goldens moved by at most 2 levels (`filters_x2`, `shapes_x1`, `shapes_x2`). Filter layers are still rendered again by every band that their reach crosses. With radius/2, drop-shadow-2xl in indigo was 5.1 mean and 46 max levels from Chromium; with the radius 0.19 and 2 (every drop-shadow preset within 2 levels away from edges); the Burst of `examples/shapes` (`drop-shadow-lg`) has a wider glow (goldens up to 35 levels)
