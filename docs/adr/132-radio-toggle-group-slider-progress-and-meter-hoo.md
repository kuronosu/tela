# ADR-132: Radio, toggle group, slider, progress and meter hooks of package `aria`

- **Date:** 2026-10-25
- **Status:** Accepted

## Decision

Radio, toggle group, slider, progress and meter hooks of package `aria` (phase 10, T3; the hooks that the kit's radio group, segmented control, slider, progress bar and meter are built on, ADR-129; every public shape is listed for the owner's review; amends §14, ADR-100, ADR-101 and ADR-104). **`UseRadioGroup[K comparable](RadioGroupOptions[K])`** (React Aria's `useRadioGroup`, `useRadio` and `useRadioGroupState`): `Keys`, `DisabledKeys`, `Value *K` (controlled), `DefaultValue *K`, `OnValueChange(K)` (ADR-101's rule, so the callback also follows an uncontrolled group; nothing is checked without a value, and a controlled value that is not a key checks nothing), `Disabled`, `ReadOnly`, `Required`, `Orientation` (`"vertical"` by default, as React Aria's), `Direction`, `AriaLabel`, `AriaLabelledBy`, `Ref`; the result has `Props` (`radiogroup`, `aria-orientation`, `aria-required`, `-readonly` and `-disabled`, the name), `LabelProps`, `RadioProps(k)`, `RadioID(k)`, `RadioRef(k)`, `IsSelected(k)`, `Value`/`HasValue` and `Ref`. A radio is `radio` + `aria-checked` + `h.ID` + `core.DefaultButtonType("button")` (ADR-106: a radio in a Form is never its default button, and a `Label(For(RadioID(k)))` names it and, on a Button, checks it through the forwarded click), with a tab stop on the checked radio, else on the first enabled one. Like the collections (ADR-104) it has one `ElementRef` per key (`core.NewElementRef`) and one hook-level `rover` (a state that the handlers write directly; the refs of keys that leave are dropped). **Rules** (React Aria's `onKeyDown` of the group): all four arrows move the focus to the next or previous enabled radio, wrapping, and check it, in either orientation, with left and right swapped only by `Direction: "rtl"` on a horizontal group; the focus moves even when the value is refused (read-only, or a controlled value that the caller does not store); Space checks (not on repeat); Enter does nothing (a Button would click). A radio is checked by its **click**, not by a press hook: a click is every press (mouse, touch, pen, the semantics click, `ElementRef.Click`, a Label's forwarded click), and a radio needs no `Pressed` state, so there is no per-item `usePressCore` (a hook cannot be called once per key); **no hidden input and no form value** (Tela has no form data for custom controls, ADR-069). **`UseToggleGroup[K comparable](ToggleGroupOptions[K])`** (React Aria's `useToggleButtonGroup`, `useToggleButtonGroupItem` and `useToolbar`): `Keys`, `DisabledKeys`, `Disabled`, `SelectionMode` (`"single"` by default, as React Aria's; `"multiple"`), `Selected`/`DefaultSelected`/`OnSelectionChange` with `aria.Selection[K]` (ADR-104's controlled rule: the zero Selection leaves it to the hook, an equal selection is not reported), `DisallowEmptySelection`, `Orientation` (`"horizontal"`), `Direction`, `ShouldFocusWrap`, `AriaLabel`, `AriaLabelledBy`, `Ref`; the result has `Props`, `ItemProps(k)`, `ItemRef(k)`, `IsSelected(k)`, `Selected` and `Ref`. As in React Aria, `"single"` is a `radiogroup` of `radio` items with `aria-checked` and `"multiple"` a `toolbar` of `button` items with `aria-pressed`; a press on the item that is on turns it off unless `DisallowEmptySelection`. The group is one tab stop that remembers the item that had the focus (else the first item that is on, else the first enabled one), the arrows of the orientation move the focus without selecting (up and down are not the horizontal group's), skipping disabled items; Enter and Space toggle on the key down. **Deviations:** Home and End jump (Radix's toggle group has them, React Aria's toolbar does not); the arrows do not wrap unless `ShouldFocusWrap` (React Aria's toolbar calls `focusNext()` without `wrap`, as far as the port could verify; Radix's `loop` defaults to true, so the kit passes it). **`UseSlider(SliderOptions)`** (React Aria's `useSlider`, `useSliderThumb` and `useSliderState`): `Values *[]float64`, `DefaultValues`, `OnValuesChange([]float64)`, `OnChangeEnd([]float64)` (the callbacks get copies), `Min` (0), `Max` (100 when both are 0; `Max <= Min` panics in dev mode), `Step` (1; a negative one panics), `MinStepsBetweenThumbs`, `Orientation`, `Direction`, `Disabled`, `FormatValue(float64) string`, `AriaLabel`, `AriaLabelledBy`, `ThumbLabels []string`; the result has `Props` (`group` and its name), `TrackProps`, `LabelProps`, `ThumbProps(i)`, `ThumbRef(i)`, `TrackRef`, `ThumbPercent(i)`, `ValueText(i)`, `IsThumbDragging(i)`, and `Values` and `Dragging` of the build. **There is no single-value convenience**: the values are a slice, so a slider with one thumb is `[]float64{v}`, which is Radix's and shadcn's shape; the thumb count is `len(*Values)`, else `len(DefaultValues)`, else one thumb at `Min`. The rules of the state are react-stately's: a value is rounded by `snapValueToStep` (a port, with `roundToStepPrecision`) between its neighbors' values (`+/- MinStepsBetweenThumbs * Step`), so thumbs never cross; a press on the track picks the nearest thumb (the lower one on a tie, the first or the last past the ends), focuses it, moves it to the point and goes on dragging it; a press on a thumb drags it by the pointer's movement (the grab offset is kept: no jump); a mouse is captured by the element that was pressed (`core.SetPointerCapture`) and touch and pen by their implicit capture, so a drag follows the pointer anywhere, and a cancel ends it; keys: the arrows move a thumb by `Step` (a page with Shift), PageUp and PageDown by a page (`max(Step, round(range/10/Step)*Step)`), Home and End to the thumb's limits, `Direction: "rtl"` mirrors left and right, and a vertical slider has `Min` at the bottom; `OnChangeEnd` follows every key that changed a value and every drag, as React Aria's `onChangeEnd` does. A thumb is `slider` with `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin` and `aria-valuemax` (its limits, the neighbors' for a range slider, as React Aria's range input), `aria-valuetext` (`FormatValue`, by default the number as it is written; locale digits are the caller's, with `i18n`, since a default from the system locale would make the output depend on the machine), `aria-orientation`, a tab stop, and a name from `ThumbLabels[i]`, else `AriaLabel`, else the element with `LabelProps`. **Dragging repaints, it does not build:** `ThumbPercent(i)` (0 to 100: React Aria's `getThumbPercent` is a fraction, and the name says percent) reads a `UseValue[int]` version counter and the hook's own state, so it is meant for the paint-phase closure of the thumb (`tela.TranslateFn(func() (x, y float32) { return float32(s.ThumbPercent(i)) / 100 * track.Get().Width, 0 })`, with `track` the `tela.UseSize(s.TrackRef)`); a pointer move of an uncontrolled slider updates the state at once and `core.PublishValue`s the counter (ADR-096: the paint readers are evaluated again in that frame), and the component builds when the drag starts, when it ends and when the pointer has rested for 120 ms (`core.UseTimeout`), so that `aria-valuenow` and the semantics catch up without a build per move. A controlled slider shows what the caller passes (a build publishes the counter when the values changed, so the thumb is right in the same frame) and builds as often as the caller's state changes; a change that the caller refuses leaves the thumb where it was. **Measured** (`TestSliderDragDoesNotBuildPerMove`, 20 moves of one drag): 2 builds (start and end), 2 layouts, a repaint per move, and the thumb is under the pointer after every move. Touch and pen drag like a mouse; `touch-action` is not supported (§10), so a touch drag inside a scroller can become a scroll and be cancelled. **`UseProgressBar(ProgressBarOptions)` and `UseMeter(MeterOptions)`** (React Aria's `useProgressBar` and `useMeter`): `Value`, `Min`, `Max` (as the slider's), `FormatValue`, `AriaLabel`, `AriaLabelledBy` and, for the bar, `Indeterminate` (no `aria-valuenow` or `-valuetext`, `Percent` 0); the result (`ProgressBarResult`) has `Props` (`progressbar` or `meter`, the range and the text), `LabelProps`, `Percent` (0 to 100, again not React Aria's fraction) and `ValueText` (the rounded percentage, `"40%"`, unless `FormatValue`). The meter's role is `meter`, where React Aria writes `"meter progressbar"` (a fallback list that Tela's `Role` does not take). **Aria snapshot** (as Playwright 1.61 prints the equivalent HTML): `radiogroup "Plan"` with `radio "Pro" [checked]`; `radiogroup` or `toolbar` with `radio [checked]` or `button [pressed]` items; `group "Volume"` with `slider "Volume"` (no value: the snapshot prints the values of text fields only); `progressbar "Upload"`; `meter "Disk usage"`. **Names the owner has not seen:** `UseRadioGroup`, `RadioGroupOptions`, `RadioGroupResult`, `RadioProps`, `RadioID`, `RadioRef`, `DefaultValue *K`, `OnValueChange`, `UseToggleGroup`, `ToggleGroupOptions`, `ToggleGroupResult`, `ItemProps`, `ItemRef`, `DisallowEmptySelection`, `ShouldFocusWrap` (also a `ListBoxOptions` name), `UseSlider`, `SliderOptions`, `SliderResult`, `TrackProps`, `TrackRef`, `ThumbProps`, `ThumbRef`, `ThumbPercent`, `ThumbLabels`, `ValueText`, `IsThumbDragging`, `OnValuesChange`, `OnChangeEnd`, `MinStepsBetweenThumbs`, `FormatValue`, `UseProgressBar`, `UseMeter`, `ProgressBarOptions`, `MeterOptions`, `ProgressBarResult`, `Percent`, `Indeterminate`

## Rationale

The kit's radio group, segmented control, slider, progress bar and meter need behavior that is right once (keys, focus, roles, drag, rounding) and a state that the rules of ADR-101 and ADR-104 already describe, so the hooks reuse them (`*K`, `*[]float64`, `Selection`) instead of inventing a state model. A radio is checked by a click and a toggle item toggles on one because a hook is called once per group and the engine's click already is every press. The slider is where a hook can protect the frame budget, since a drag has to follow the pointer at the display rate: reading a Value in a paint closure and publishing it in the frame (ADR-096) makes the drag cost a repaint, which the measurement shows, while the 120 ms rebuild keeps what assistive technology and tests read honest. Keeping React Aria's rounding, neighbor limits and closest-thumb rule means that a range slider behaves as the reference does with every pointer and key. Rendering is unchanged: every golden of the repository is bit-identical
